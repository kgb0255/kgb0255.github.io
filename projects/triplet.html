General Relativistic Triplet Interaction of Pulsational Eigenmodes of Neutron Stars

Neutron stars are among the most compact objects in the universe, with radii only a few times their Schwarzschild radius, and so they are inherently general-relativistic. As a star's compactness increases, Newtonian and general-relativistic stellar models diverge sharply: their predicted mass-radius relations can differ by more than 20% for realistic neutron-star parameters. Since gravitational-wave observations are sensitive to a star's internal structure, a theory of stellar oscillations aimed at precision inference cannot treat general relativity as a small correction — it must be built into the eigenmode basis from the start.

A second complication arises over the course of a binary inspiral. Early on, the tidal field of a companion star deforms each star only slightly, and linear perturbation theory is an excellent approximation. But as the stars spiral closer together and the tidal field strengthens, this deformation grows large enough that the response becomes nonlinear: individual oscillation modes no longer evolve independently. As the two neutron stars coalesce and emit gravitational waves, this nonlinearity leaves an imprint on the signal, modifying its phase evolution in ways that linear models cannot capture.

The lowest-order nonlinearity comes from a mode's triplet coupling to two other modes together with nonlinear tidal forcing, which I calculate in this work within a fully general-relativistic framework. The goal is to determine how relativistic gravity reshapes this nonlinear mode coupling, and what signature it leaves on the gravitational-wave signal emitted by coalescing neutron star binaries.
